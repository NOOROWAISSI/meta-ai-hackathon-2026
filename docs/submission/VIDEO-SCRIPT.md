# NextStep: three-minute demo

Target: 3:00 maximum, approximately 365 spoken words. Rehearse the real sequence once before recording. Use the final reviewed integration build and synthetic demonstration identities. Do not describe a synthetic provider as live Meta. A locally reachable application is not a public hosted deployment.

## 0:00–0:20 — The problem

Show Discover with one current, source-backed opportunity.

> Students do not just need more links. They need to understand which opportunities are worth their time, what they could gain, and what to do next. NextStep connects real opportunities with a student's confirmed experience, then turns a chosen opportunity into a practical preparation plan.

## 0:20–0:50 — Discover and inspect

Choose one university/community filter, open internal opportunity details, show its original source, then save it.

> Here, a student explores opportunities from official organizations and university communities. They can filter the results, save an interesting opportunity, and read its details before leaving the application. Dates and sources stay visible. Past activities and unconfirmed announcements are separated from current opportunities. Missing information stays unknown instead of becoming an invented deadline or requirement.

## 0:50–1:35 — Experience and Meta comparison

Show an already imported synthetic CV or pinned GitHub source, one original passage, a student-confirmed contribution, and the saved comparison. Avoid waiting for an import during recording.

> A shared interest tag is not enough to judge whether an opportunity adds value. The student can import a CV or selected GitHub material, review the extracted text, and explicitly allow analysis. Meta proposes experience facts with supporting passages. The student decides what to confirm and explains their personal contribution. A repository link is not proof of authorship or ability.
>
> NextStep then compares those confirmed facts with this specific opportunity. It explains the potential benefit, what may already be familiar, and what needs clarification. Unreported knowledge is not automatically treated as a failed requirement. These recommendations are separate from the organizer's participation rules.

## 1:35–2:35 — Plan, accept and progress

Show available hours and goal, generation disclosure, a candidate, explicit acceptance, one task drawer, Done, and reload. Use a real validated response or clearly label a synthetic replay. Budget generation latency in the rehearsal; do not hide a failure behind a fabricated success.

> Once the student chooses to prepare, their goals and available time shape a proposed plan. Before sharing context with Meta, the application explains what will be sent and asks for acknowledgment.
>
> Generated work is a draft. The student reviews the tasks, effort and capacity warning before accepting it. The backend validates the response, assigns task identities, and stores the accepted plan in MySQL.
>
> Here is a task's deliverable, completion criteria and any available reviewed resources. I mark this task Done, save, and reload. The progress remains. Completing a learning task does not automatically prove submission compliance or eligibility. Changes to the student's context flag older results without erasing confirmed progress.

## 2:35–3:00 — Close

Return to the opportunity, then My Plans.

> Meta helps interpret experience, explain an opportunity's value, and propose preparation actions. NextStep surrounds that assistance with source visibility, student confirmation, explicit plan acceptance and persistent progress. The result is a clear path from discovering an opportunity to taking the next useful step.
